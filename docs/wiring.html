Nexys 4 DDR to HUB75

Twelve signals, two buffer chips, one breadboard, and a 5 V supply that does not go anywhere near the FPGA board. Everything below is what it takes to get the hub75 core driving a real panel.

01

Why the two buffer chips

The Artix-7's Pmod pins are 3.3 V LVCMOS. The panel's input buffer is a 74HC245 running off 5 V, and an HC part guarantees nothing about an input below 3.5 V. So 3.3 V straight into the panel is out of spec — it works on a short cable at room temperature and then fails at random, which is the worst way for it to fail.

A 74AHCT245 fixes it in one part. The HCT input threshold is 2.0 V, so 3.3 V is a solid high; the chip runs off 5 V, so its outputs swing the full 5 V the panel wants. Twelve signals need two of them: one octal carries the eight from Pmod JB, the second carries the four from JC with its spare inputs grounded.

The panel's current never touches the breadboard A 32×16 panel draws up to 2 A at full white. Breadboard rails and their spring contacts are not built for that. The 5 V supply feeds the panel directly, through the panel's own power cable, and only a thin tap goes to the breadboard for the two buffer chips — which need about 10 mA between them.

04

Breadboard layout

Both chips sit across the centre channel, notch to the left. Everything below the chips is the 3.3 V side going back to the Nexys; everything above is the 5 V side going out to the panel.

Pmod pin numbering

A Pmod header is 2×6. The top row is pins 1–4 signal, 5 ground, 6 3.3 V; the bottom row is 7–10 signal, 11 ground, 12 3.3 V. So JB's eight signals are 1, 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 9, 10 — not 1 through 8. Take your ground from pin 5 or 11, and the 10 kΩ pull-up's top end from JC pin 6.

05

Build it

  1. Everything off

    Nexys unplugged from USB, 5 V supply unplugged from the wall. You will be moving wires next to a rail that can push 3 A.

  2. Seat the chips and the rails

    Both 74AHCT245s across the centre channel, notch to the left, with a few free rows between them. Then bridge the breadboard's power rails if your board splits them at the middle, and drop a 0.1 µF cap across pins 10 and 20 of each chip — physically across, not off at the end of the rail.

  3. Wire the static pins

    Four per chip, and they never change:

    • pin 20 (VCC) and pin 1 (DIR) → +5 V rail. DIR high selects A→B, which is the direction we want.
    • pin 10 (GND) and pin 19 (/OE) → GND rail. /OE low enables the outputs permanently.
    • On U2 only: pins 6, 7, 8, 9 → GND rail. Unused CMOS inputs must not float; floating ones oscillate and heat the chip.

  4. Add the OE pull-up

    10 kΩ from U2 pin 5 to JC pin 6 (3.3 V), not to the 5 V rail — pulling an unconfigured FPGA pin above its own supply is how you find its ESD clamp.

    This is the part that stops the panel flashing garbage between power-on and configuration. OE is active low, the FPGA's pins are high-Z until the bitstream loads, and without the pull-up the buffer input floats: the panel could come up fully lit at 2 A on an undriven row. With it, the panel is blanked until the FPGA says otherwise.

  5. Ground the Nexys to the breadboard

    JB pin 5 → GND rail, and JC pin 5 → GND rail. Do this before the signal wires, so that there is never a moment where a signal has a path but its return does not.

  6. Twelve signal wires in

    Pmod to the A side, per the drawing: JB1–4 and JB7–10 to U1 pins 2–9, JC1–4 to U2 pins 2–5. Count twice — the JB numbering skips 5 and 6.

  7. Twelve signal wires out

    B side to the panel's input header — the one marked with an arrow pointing away from it, usually JIN1. Watch the reversed B numbering: pin 18 is B1, pin 11 is B8. Panel pin 12 (D) stays unconnected; this is a 1/8 scan panel with three address lines.

    Take at least one ground across as well, from the GND rail to panel pin 4, 8 or 16.

  8. Power, in the right order

    • 5 V supply → the panel's own power connector, directly, with its supplied cable.
    • A thin pair from the same supply terminals → the breadboard's +5 V and GND rails.
    • 100 µF across the breadboard rails, watching the polarity.

    The supply's ground now reaches the panel, the breadboard and — through step 05 — the Nexys. That is the common ground the first drawing marks.

  9. Check before you energise anything

    • Continuity: +5 V rail to GND rail reads open, not a few ohms.
    • Nothing connects Pmod pin 6 or 12 (3.3 V) to the 5 V rail — except through the 10 kΩ.
    • Both chips: pin 1 high, pin 19 low, pin 20 on 5 V, pin 10 on ground.
    • Both chips seated across the channel, not shorted along one row.
    • The panel lead is in the input header.
    • The electrolytic's stripe is on the ground side.

  10. Power up and program

    5 V supply on first — the panel should stay dark. If it lights up before the FPGA is programmed, kill it and re-check step 04. Then plug in the Nexys over USB and load the bitstream:

    • make bit, then Vivado's hardware manager; or
    • openFPGALoader -b nexys_a7_100 build/top_nexys4ddr.bit

    Expect LD0 blinking about once a second, LD1 lit, and a slowly drifting colour gradient across all 512 pixels.

06

When it doesn't work

Panel dark, LD0 blinking
The core is running, so this is the wiring. Check OE first (U2 pin 5 → pin 15 → panel 15): a stuck-high OE blanks everything while the rest of the design works perfectly.
Panel dark, LD0 not blinking
The bitstream is not loaded or the board is not running. Nothing to do with the panel yet.
Lit before programming
The OE pull-up is missing, on the wrong pin, or tied to 5 V instead of 3.3 V.
Image mirrored left to right
Your panel fills its shift register from the other end. Count col downwards in hub75_scan.vhd; the header says where.
Split or doubled vertically
An address line. A, B, C must reach panel pins 9, 10, 11 in that order — and check none of them landed on pin 12.
Only one half works
R1/G1/B1 and R2/G2/B2 are crossed, or U1's B side got read left to right instead of 18-down-to-11.
Colours wrong but stable
Two data wires swapped. The order across U1 is R1 G1 B1 R2 G2 B2 on pins 2–7.
Works dim, garbles when bright
The 5 V rail is sagging. Either the supply is undersized or the panel's current is going through the breadboard.
Intermittent, worse on long wires
Ringing on CLK. Shorten the leads first; 33–100 Ω in series with CLK and LAT at the buffer output is the next step.
